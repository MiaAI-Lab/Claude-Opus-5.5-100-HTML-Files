=== PROMPT (exact prompt used) ===
Create "Alpenglow — A Day in the Mountains", a parallax landscape that cycles through a full day. Six procedurally generated mountain ridge layers (midpoint displacement) with atmospheric perspective (farther = lighter and bluer), a foreground lake reflecting sky and mountains with gentle ripples, pine silhouettes on the nearest ridge, and birds that occasionally cross. Time of day animates (or is scrubbed with a slider): pre-dawn indigo, sunrise with pink alpenglow on the peaks (#ff9eaa, #ffd6a5), bright noon blues, golden hour, sunset reds, dusk violets, and a starry night with a moon and the occasional meteor. The sun and moon follow arcs; sky gradients interpolate smoothly between keyframes; ridge colours are recomputed for each moment. Interaction: pointer parallax; a time slider with icons; "Play day" (60 seconds); clicking the sky regenerates the mountains from a new seed. Typography: a small, elegant sans showing the current time and a poetic name for each phase ("Blue hour").

=== DESCRIPTION ===
Alpenglow is a full-screen canvas landscape that lives through a whole day, in a quiet, graphic style. Six mountain ridges are generated by midpoint displacement and recede from dark forested hills to tall, snow-capped peaks, each layer lighter and bluer with distance. Beneath them, a still lake mirrors the scene with gentle ripples. Pines line the nearest shores, a reed-fringed bank frames the lower left, and birds occasionally cross by day. The first frame opens at sunrise, as pink alpenglow (#ff9eaa, #ffd6a5) catches the summits over a lavender valley. From there the day plays through in 60 seconds: bright noon blues, golden hour, sunset reds, violet dusk, and a starry night with a moon and the occasional meteor, before blue hour returns. Small letter-spaced sans type shows the time, a poetic name for the phase ("Blue hour", "Afterglow") and a line of description. A glass dock holds the time slider, whose track is painted with the day's own sky colours and marked with moon, sunrise, sun and sunset icons.

=== MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES ===
- Midpoint-displacement ridges (513 samples each) with per-layer roughness and peak sharpening, a gaussian "signature summit" lift and renormalisation, redrawn every frame from a seed
- Atmospheric perspective: each ridge colour is mixed from near and far tones and then toward the horizon haze according to depth, with a vertical gradient that pales toward the valley floor
- Low-poly light and shadow: a smoothed, wide-window slope finds whole faces turned away from the sun (which side depends on whether the sun is east or west), and each face becomes a triangular shadow falling from its high end; on snow the same faces turn cool blue
- Ragged snowlines from a second noise field, snow tinted by the light of the moment, and an alpenglow gradient laid over the upper faces at sunrise, sunset and afterglow
- Twelve colour keyframes (sky top, middle and horizon, haze, near and far rock, glow, snow, water, cloud, star visibility) smoothstep-interpolated for any time of day
- The sun and moon travel on arcs between rise and set. The sun's disc and halo warm as it sinks; the moon is drawn gibbous with craters and a halo; a low sun warms the horizon band
- Twinkling tinted stars, meteors with gradient tails, cloud wisps made from a tinted soft sprite, morning and evening mist on the water, and small flocks of flapping birds
- The lake is built from 2-pixel strips of the world above, flipped and shifted by two travelling sine waves whose amplitude grows toward the viewer, then tinted and darkened, with faint shimmer bands, a glitter path under the sun (or the moon at night) and a shoreline highlight
- Pointer parallax with eased offsets per layer (from 8% for the far range to 80% for the nearest, with the bank strongest), mirrored correctly in the reflection
- New ranges rise out of the lake layer by layer with a staggered smoothstep animation

=== INTERACTION MODEL ===
- Move the pointer (or drag on touch) for parallax depth
- Click open sky to generate a new range from a new seed; the "New range" button and the N key do the same, and the range number is shown bottom-left
- The time slider scrubs through all 24 hours (and pauses playback). "Play day" / "Pause" (or Space) runs the full cycle in 60 seconds, and "Now" jumps to the real local time
- The clock, phase name, colour-matched phase label and descriptive line update continuously. A polite live region announces each new phase, and the slider exposes aria-valuetext
- Drawing pauses with the tab (requestAnimationFrame) and the clock resumes cleanly. Reduced motion starts paused, damps the ripples and reeds, and skips meteors and birds
