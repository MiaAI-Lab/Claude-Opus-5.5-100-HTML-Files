Floor III

The Salon

On the last night of 1928, the brass doors of The Aurelian opened onto a lobby of black lacquer and emerald, and an orchestra that did not stop playing until the milk carts were out. Nearly a century later the Salon still keeps that hour: the fans on its walls unfold at dusk, the lift dial still ticks from floor to floor, and every chevron is polished by hand before the first guest comes down.

The building was drawn as a single idea — a sunburst climbing a ziggurat — and nothing was allowed to break its symmetry. Even the elevator needle swings the same distance left and right of centre.

  1. 1928

    Opening night

    New Year's Eve. Nine hundred guests, one orchestra, and a ribbon cut with the architect's drafting knife.

  2. 1936

    The ceiling is re-gilded

    Six gilders spend a winter on scaffolds laying four thousand leaves over the Salon's sunburst.

  3. 1974

    Named a landmark

    The city protects the tower, its lacquer lobby and its brass lift dials from any change at all.

  4. Today

    Restored, not renewed

    A four-year restoration returns every sconce, fan and needle to working order — nothing replaced that could be mended.

The Aurelian, its history and its guests are a work of fiction.
