A pattern that knits itself

Woolgathering

Conway's Game of Life, worked in stockinette. Every generation is a new row: a stitch is knitted in yarn when exactly three neighbours are yarn, it stays on the needle with two or three, and otherwise it slips back into the ground colour.

Stockinette, flat. 49 stitches × 45 rows, worked in four shades. Press play, or draw on the fabric to add your own stitches.

Space play or pause · N one row · R randomise · C clear · 1–4 motifs. On the focused fabric, arrow keys move and Enter toggles a stitch.

The Game of Life was devised by the mathematician John Horton Conway and introduced to the world in Martin Gardner's Scientific American column in October 1970. The glider gun is Bill Gosper's discovery from the same year: it fires a new glider every thirty generations.

Woolgathering Mills and its ball band are invented for this page. No sheep were consulted.
