=== DESCRIPTION ===
Hi-Fi Walnut is a private listening room rendered as a luxury object. On deep green felt (#1f3b2d) lit by a warm lamp sits a top-down walnut turntable: a figured veneer plinth (#5c3d2e) with a brass inlay line, hinges and nameplate, a brushed-aluminium platter with strobe markings and an amber strobe lamp, a black record with fine grooves, dark silent bands exactly where the tracks change, and fixed specular light arcs that stay put while the record and its label turn underneath. An S-shaped aluminium tonearm with a brass counterweight lifts, swings onto the lead-in, lowers with a needle-drop thump and then tracks inward through the side. Below the turntable an integrated amplifier holds two cream analogue VU meters that glow amber and swing with the music, and a knurled brass volume knob. On the right a cream liner-notes card (#efe6d8) carries generative CSS cover art, album titles in display-serif small caps, the track list, a brass progress rail, the transport and a crate of three records. All three fictional albums are ambient pieces generated live with Web Audio: the candlelit Rhodes, flute and brushes of "Moss & Lantern Light", the rainy organ and electric piano of "Tidewater Hours", and the music-box bell arpeggios of "Brass Constellations".

=== MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES ===
- Walnut veneer from inline SVG: an x-stretched feTurbulence grain mapped through feColorMatrix into walnut tones, a second low-frequency turbulence for darker figure, then lamp-light, bevel and brass inlay layers
- Record built from stacked CSS gradients: px-pitch repeating-radial grooves, smooth lead-in, run-out and rim bands, faint asymmetric pressing sheen, dust specks, and two dark inter-track bands whose radii (CSS custom properties) are computed from each album's real track timing
- Fixed specular "bowtie" highlights: a masked conic-gradient in screen blend mode that never rotates, so the grooves appear to turn beneath the light
- Rotating per-album SVG labels with text on circular paths (title above, imprint below), a static spindle, and a platter with repeating-conic strobe dots lit by an amber lamp
- Tonearm geometry solved from the pivot, arm length and spindle distance (θ = acos(k/R) − φ), so the stylus sits on the exact groove radius for the current album time; a blurred shadow twin and the cue lever respond to the lift height
- Platter inertia: angular velocity eases up to 33⅓ rpm (200°/s) and coasts down slowly
- Record swap with the Web Animations API: the disc lifts, spins away off the deck, the label and bands change, and the next record slides in and settles
- VU meters in SVG with a true VU scale (needle position linear in voltage, −20 to +3 VU with a red zone), spring ballistics tuned to roughly 300 ms, peak lamps and a warm backlight
- Generative album art in pure CSS: lantern glow with moss hills, tide bands with moon and rain, and brass orbits with a star field and SVG constellations
- A felt texture painted once on a fixed canvas from thousands of short fibres, plus lamp light and vignette

=== INTERACTION MODEL ===
- Play/Pause from the big brass button, a click on the record, the deck's START·STOP button or Space: the motor spins up, the arm cues and drops, and the music starts; pause lifts the arm in place and the platter coasts to a stop
- Previous/Next (buttons or P/N) lift the arm, move it to the next track band and drop it again; tracks can also be chosen from the list, and the progress rail can be dragged or clicked to seek (←/→ jump 10 s)
- Change the record from the crate (or keys 1 2 3), with the full lift-away and settle animation; if music was playing it resumes on the new record
- Brass volume knob: drag vertically or horizontally, use the mouse wheel, or arrow and Page keys (it is an ARIA slider); ↑/↓ also adjust volume globally, and a MUTE toggle switch (or M) silences output
- Audio: an AudioContext created on the first gesture; FM electric piano, flute with breath noise and delayed vibrato, inharmonic FM bells, a detuned-saw organ pad, a sine/triangle bass and brushed noise, all through gentle saturation, a glue compressor and a generated convolution reverb; wow at the platter's own 0.556 Hz, looping surface crackle with a scratch that ticks once per revolution, and rain for the grey record; bars are seeded, so seeking replays the same music
- End of side: the stylus drifts into the run-out groove, thumps softly each revolution, then the arm returns automatically
- prefers-reduced-motion shortens arm and swap animations; the frame loop pauses while the tab is hidden
