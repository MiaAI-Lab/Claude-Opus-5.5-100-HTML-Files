Long Water

The world's great rivers

A river's length is a slippery number. Follow a different headwater, trace another channel through the delta, or survey in a wet year instead of a dry one, and the total shifts by hundreds of kilometres. These are the commonly cited, approximate figures for Earth's ten longest rivers — each drawn to the same scale, source on the left, sea on the right.

Lengths are commonly cited approximations and sources vary: a river's measured length depends on which headwater you follow, how finely you trace its meanders and delta channels, and the resolution of the map. Some surveys trace the Amazon to a more distant Andean source and rank it first — the Nile's crown is conventional rather than settled. Discharge figures are rounded long-term means near the mouth.

No European river makes the list: the Volga, Europe's longest, runs about 3,530 km.
