The Sunlight Zone

Epipelagic · 0 – 200 m

Light enough for photosynthesis. It is only a thin skin on the ocean, yet it holds most of the sea life people ever see.

The Twilight Zone

Mesopelagic · 200 – 1,000 m

Sunlight fades to a faint blue, then to nothing. Many animals here make their own light, and many rise toward the surface each night to feed.

The Midnight Zone

Bathypelagic · 1,000 – 4,000 m

No sunlight reaches this deep. The water hovers around 4 °C, and the only light is made by living things.

The Abyssal Zone

Abyssopelagic · 4,000 – 6,000 m

Near-freezing, pitch-black water above vast plains of soft sediment. The pressure is hundreds of times greater than at the surface.

The Hadal Zone

Hadalpelagic · 6,000 – 10,935 m

Named for Hades, the Greek underworld. Depths like these exist only in ocean trenches, most of them around the rim of the Pacific.

10,935m

Challenger Deep · Mariana Trench

The deepest known point of the ocean floor, where the water presses down with over a thousand times the pressure at the surface. In 1960 Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh became the first people to reach it.
